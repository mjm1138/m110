## Targets That Play to the S50's Strengths

The S50's key advantages are its **50mm aperture** (best light gathering in its class) and **longer 250mm focal length**, making it ideal for:
- Faint objects that need light-gathering power
- Moderately sized targets that fit its ~0.7° × 1.3° field of view
- High-contrast planetary nebulae and compact galaxies

### 🌌 Deep Sky — March Evening Sky (Visible mid-evening, looking south/southeast)

#### **M42 — Orion Nebula** ⭐ First light recommendation
- **Why S50:** Bright enough to show stunning detail even from suburban skies; the S50's aperture brings out the Trapezium cluster and nebula structure beautifully
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Best time:** Early evening in March, still well-placed in the southwest after dark
- **Note:** Large and bright — excellent for your first session to confirm alignment and focus

#### **M78 — Reflection Nebula (Orion)**
- **Why S50:** Fainter than M42 — this is where the aperture advantage really shows versus 30mm scopes
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Best time:** Early evening, near M42

#### **M1 — Crab Nebula (Taurus)**
- **Why S50:** A supernova remnant — faint and compact, exactly the kind of target where 50mm aperture earns its keep
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Best time:** High in the sky in early evening March

#### **M81 / M82 — Bode's Galaxy & Cigar Galaxy (Ursa Major)** ⭐ Highly recommended
- **Why S50:** Two galaxies fit in one frame with the S50's FOV; M82's irregular starburst structure shows well with good aperture; these are circumpolar from Boulder so available all night
- **Difficulty:** Beginner–Intermediate
- **Best time:** High in the north, excellent all night in March

#### **Leo Triplet — M65, M66, NGC 3628**
- **Why S50:** Three galaxies in one frame, rising to good altitude by mid-evening in late March; the S50's focal length is well-matched to the angular size of these galaxies
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Best time:** Late March, rises well after 9pm MST
- **Note:** Wait until late March when Leo is higher in the sky for best results

#### **M97 — Owl Nebula (Ursa Major)**
- **Why S50:** A planetary nebula — compact, needs aperture to show the "owl eye" structure; circumpolar from Boulder
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Best time:** All night in March

#### **M104 — Sombrero Galaxy (Virgo)**
- **Why S50:** The dark dust lane across this galaxy is one of the most iconic sights in a smart telescope; S50's aperture helps reveal it
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Best time:** Rising in the southeast late evening, better in April but usable late March after midnight

### ☀️ Solar Imaging

The S50 is the best performer among the budget smart scopes for solar detail due to its aperture advantage. Boulder's frequent sunny days make this a great daytime activity while you wait for clear nights.

- **Sunspot detail:** The S50 will show sunspot groups, umbra/penumbra structure, and active regions well
- **Solar filter:** Included — confirm it's properly attached before every session
- **Best practice:** Image in the morning when atmospheric turbulence ("seeing") is typically lower
- **We are currently near Solar Maximum (Solar Cycle 25)** — excellent time to observe; expect frequent active regions and sunspot groups

### 🌕 Lunar Imaging

- **Best nights for lunar imaging:** March 20–24 (waxing crescent to first quarter) — the terminator (shadow line) creates dramatic crater relief
- **First Quarter** (around March 25) is the classic sweet spot — half the Moon in shadow, maximizing 3D crater detail
- The S50's longer focal length gives more magnification than the S30, showing finer crater and rille detail
- Recommended targets on the terminator: **Tycho**, **Clavius**, **Copernicus**, **Valles Marineris region**

## Tips for Your First Sessions

1. **Let the scope cool down** for 20–30 minutes outside before imaging — thermal equilibration improves image quality
2. **Start with M42 or M81/M82** — bright, forgiving targets to learn the app on
3. **Check Clear Dark Sky the afternoon of your session** — conditions can change fast in Boulder
4. **Stack more frames on faint targets** — the S50 rewards patience; longer sessions on galaxies reveal more structure
5. **Boulder light pollution:** You're in a suburban/urban environment (Bortle 5–6 roughly). The S50's aperture helps fight this, but consider a short drive toward Nederland or Flagstaff Mountain on your best nights for noticeably darker skies
