Your telescopes — the Devices page

← Back to the guide

The Devices page (the last entry on the rail, Ctrl+6) has one entry for every telescope you've imported from. M110 learns a telescope from the TELESCOP card in its FITS headers, so there's nothing to register: import from it once and it appears here. Two units of the same model are two entries (each has its own serial), but they count together toward an object's integration goal.

The list is ordered by most recent capture. Pick a telescope to see its details on the right.

Identity

  • Nickname — call it what you call it ("Backyard S50", "Travel S30"). The nickname is used everywhere the telescope is named: the session tables, Planning's Telescope picker, field guides. Save / Revert light up once you've changed something.
  • Model — M110 recognises Seestar and DwarfLab telescopes from their headers and says so. For anything else, tell it the model here so it gets the right planning limits and goal scaling.
  • Retired — hides a telescope you no longer use from the list and from the pickers. Its captures stay in your Library. Show retired brings it back into the list.

Planning limits

Use for planning makes this the telescope the Planning page plans for. You can also switch telescopes there. Each model comes with its own limits, which you can adjust per telescope:

  • Start ceiling — the highest altitude a capture may start at. A Seestar refuses to start near the zenith, so its ceiling is hard; a Dwarf's is softer.
  • Low-altitude floor, Longest exposure, Default exposure, Gain.

Save limits keeps your changes; Model defaults puts the model's values back.

Optics

Aperture, focal length, f-ratio and pixel size, and what they mean for your goals: "Integration goals here are ×0.73 the S50's" for a faster telescope, for example. A recognised model shows its published specs, read-only. For a telescope M110 doesn't recognise you can enter them yourself (Save optics); until you do, its goals use the S50's numbers.

What it captured

  • Objects — every object this telescope has shot, with its integration time and last capture. Right-click → Open object jumps to it in the Library.
  • Captures — every capture session with its sensor temperature and the Dark it used (see below).
  • Sensor health — two charts built from its darks: the dark level (median ADU) and the hot-pixel count, each against sensor temperature.

The dark library (Seestar)

A Seestar keeps its own master darks and subtracts one from every sub before saving it. When you import from the telescope, M110 copies those masters into Calibration/<telescope>/darks/ (see Ingest) and lists them here, each with a preview, its median level and hot-pixel count. A dark is named by when the telescope made it and at what temperature ("Oct 15 21:52 · 13.9 °C"). It's named the same way everywhere in M110.

M110 never applies these darks again: that would subtract the dark twice. They're here so you can see which dark each capture got:

  • Select a capture and the dark(s) it used highlight; select a dark and right-click → Select the captures it served.
  • The Dark column shows how far the sensor drifted from that dark ("24.9 °C · Δ10.5 ⚠"). Past about 3 °C, hot pixels come out over- or under-subtracted; m110-stack --max-dark-gap 3 leaves those frames out (see Processing).
  • The Seestar switches to a better-matched dark mid-capture once the sensor has moved about 2.5 °C. A capture that switched shows each dark it used ("24.9→23.2→13.9 °C").
  • The Seestar doesn't record which dark it used, so M110 works it out from when each dark was made and the sensor temperature. It says whether a link is confirmed or inferred.

Right-click a dark → Reveal in Finder (Reveal in file manager on Windows and Linux) to see the file; right-click a capture to reveal the dark it used.
